---
title: Validating your feed
description: Check your feed with the W3C validator and understand its warnings.
---

Validate every feed before you publish it. Readers vary in how strict they are,
and a validator catches problems early.

## Use the W3C validator

The [W3C Feed Validation Service](https://validator.w3.org/feed/) accepts a
public URL or pasted XML.

1. Generate your feed and serve it, or copy the output of `feed.generate()`.
2. Open the validator and use "Validate by Direct Input" for local output, or
   "Validate by URI" for a deployed feed.
3. Fix errors first, then read the recommendations.

## The self-link warning

If you leave out `feedURL`, the validator reports:

```text
Missing atom:link with rel="self"
```

This is a recommendation, not an error. The self link tells readers the
canonical URL of the feed. Fix it by passing `feedURL`:

```ts
new Feed(channel, { feedURL: "https://example.com/rss.xml" });
```

The value should match the URL the feed is actually served from. If the two
differ, the validator warns about that too.

## Other common reports

- **Invalid email in `author`.** RSS expects `email (Name)`. A bare name is
  flagged.
- **Invalid date.** An invalid `Date` is written as `Invalid Date`. Validate
  dates before you pass them in.
- **Wrong content type.** Serve the feed as
  `application/rss+xml; charset=utf-8`. See the
  [Nuxt and Nitro guide](/docs/guides/nuxt-nitro-route).
- **Nested elements missing.** `image`, `textInput`, `skipHours` and `skipDays`
  are not yet written in the nested form RSS 2.0 requires.

The last three are covered in [troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).
